Practical information

Venue

The conference and the Training School both take place at the University of Belgrade, Faculty of Political Science, Jove Ilića 165, 11042 Belgrade, in the Voždovac district.

From Slavija Square, take tram or bus 9, 10, 14 or 33 and get off at Petrovačka.

For any question about the conference or your stay, write to the organisers through the contact form.

Visas and entry

  • Serbia is not in the Schengen area, so bring your passport. EU and EEA citizens can also enter with a national identity card.
  • Citizens of the EU and EEA, the United Kingdom, the United States, Canada and several other countries need no visa for stays of up to 90 days in any 180.
  • Other nationalities may need a visa. It is waived for holders of a valid multiple-entry Schengen, UK or US visa, or of a residence permit from one of those areas. Everyone else applies for an e-Visa at welcometoserbia.gov.rs.
  • The visa page of the Serbian Ministry of Foreign Affairs sets out the rules for each nationality.
  • Hotels register foreign guests with the police. If you rent a private flat, ask your host to register you.

From the airport

Nikola Tesla Airport (BEG) lies about 18 km west of the city centre.

A1 minibus
The easiest option leaves from outside Arrivals about every 20 minutes and reaches Slavija Square in 30 to 40 minutes. The fare is about 400 dinars, paid on board.
Bus 72
This free city bus runs to Zeleni Venac in the centre and takes 35 to 40 minutes or longer. For late arrivals, night bus 72N runs into the centre.
Taxi
Buy a fixed-price voucher at the taxi desk in the arrivals hall, hand it to the driver and pay at the end of the ride. Expect roughly 3,000 to 3,500 dinars (25 to 30 euros) to the centre. Avoid unmetered taxis and drivers who approach you in the hall.

Getting around

  • All city buses, trams and trolleybuses, and the BG Voz suburban trains, are free for everyone.
  • Google Maps, Moovit or a similar app shows live routes and times.
  • Night buses run on the main routes after midnight.
  • Take only taxis with a roof sign and a running meter, and never agree a price off the meter. CarGo, Pink Taxi, Lux Taxi, Naxis Taxi and Gold Taxi Beograd are reliable, and Uber does not operate in Belgrade.

Where to stay

The faculty lies outside the old centre. Vračar, around Slavija Square, is close by, and Stari Grad (Republic Square and Knez Mihailova) is livelier in the evening. Voždovac, the faculty's own district, has a few simple guesthouses.

Indicative 2026 prices per night are 50 to 80 euros for budget rooms, 80 to 130 euros mid-range and over 130 euros upscale. The organisers have no arrangement with any of these hotels.

Money

  • The currency is the Serbian dinar (RSD), at about 117 dinars to the euro.
  • Visa, Mastercard and contactless payment are widely accepted. Carry some cash for small shops and taxis.
  • ATMs are in the airport arrivals hall and all over town.
  • Euros are not normally accepted as payment, although some hotels quote their prices in euros.

Good to know

  • Belgrade is on Central European Summer Time (UTC+2) in June.
  • The power supply is 230 V, with plug types C and F.
  • The local language is Serbian, and English is widely spoken.
  • Emergency numbers are 112 for any emergency, 192 for the police, 193 for the fire brigade and 194 for an ambulance.
