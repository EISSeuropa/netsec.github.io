# Panelist self-service: brainstorm synthesis

> Working notes exported from the structured brainstorm that scoped [#332](https://github.com/EISSeuropa/netsec.github.io/issues/332) (reduce recurring ESSC panelist requests). Source: a multi-agent workflow (run `wk6c1rlp1`): a surface audit, five solution lenses, four persona user-path simulations, and a synthesis. Kept here as a living doc to keep building on; the shipped work is tracked in #332, #333, #335, #337.

## Synthesis

The recurring ESSC email load splits into three categories, and the right response differs per category. (1) Self-serviceable corrections (name typo, affiliation, paper title) should be DELEGATED to Indico self-edit, but only after the maintainer verifies a plain author actually sees an Edit control. The four persona simulations converge on one finding: the permission model is unverified, and shipping "you can edit it yourself" copy on an unverified assumption is worse than silence because it burns trust and adds a round-trip. (2) Self-answerable logistics (where is the printable programme, how do I register) should be PRE-EMPTED with on-page affordances and FAQ entries, because the chair persona never reaches the FAQ and only an on-page Print/Save-as-PDF control on essc-2026.html actually stops the email. (3) Genuinely human-only requests (visa invitation letters, co-author swaps, session moves) are NOT self-serviceable; the goal there is not deflection but converting a vague multi-round thread into one complete, well-routed request. The recommended mix: lead with on-page surfaces where panelists actually land (the programme page), back them with one authoritative FAQ section, make the FAQ the canonical wording to avoid drift, add a no-login/no-edit fallback that routes cleanly to the contact form, and add a visible last-synced cue so the ~24h lag does not generate "why hasn't it updated" chase emails. The visa-letter answer is a hard blocker on a maintainer policy decision and must not ship as a guess; everything else can ship without it. Principle: pre-empt what visitors can answer themselves, delegate what they can do themselves, deflect-and-route what they cannot, and reserve maintainer time for the genuine edge cases that no website measure can absorb (a consulate wants a signature, not a web page).

## Persona simulations

### A non-technical academic (e.g. an associate professor of political science) who submitted an ESSC 2026 paper months ago under their old university. They have since moved institutions. They spotted the stale affiliation on the public programme, it is roughly two weeks before the conference, they are busy, and they have no memory of any Indico password. Their mental model is "the website has my old university on it, so the person who runs the website must change it."
- **Goal:** Get the university affiliation next to their name on the public ESSC 2026 programme changed to the new one, with the least effort, ideally without creating an account or learning a new system.

## Proposed issue body (as filed, #332)

## What's happening

Every ESSC cycle the maintainer receives the same clusters of email: panelists asking for a name-typo, affiliation, or paper-title correction on the public programme; people asking where the printable or PDF programme is; international speakers asking for a visa invitation letter; and chairs asking for co-author swaps or session moves. The public site does not currently answer any of these. The FAQ has six sections but none covers conference participation; essc-2026.html has a built-in print stylesheet but no visible control or instruction telling a visitor it exists; there is zero visa-letter content anywhere in the repository; and the contact form is free-text with no topic routing.

Several of these are self-serviceable (panelists can edit their own name, affiliation, and paper title on Indico, which the daily sync reflects on the site within about a day) but the self-service path is undocumented and unverified. Persona simulations of an affiliation-changed academic, a session chair wanting a printout, a visa-required speaker, and a chair needing structural changes all predicted the visitor would still email, for reasons the current site does nothing to address.

## Why it matters

This is recurring, avoidable maintainer load concentrated in the weeks before the conference, exactly when time is scarce. Three categories need different responses. Corrections should be delegated to Indico self-edit, but only after verifying a plain author actually sees an Edit control, because shipping 'you can edit it yourself' copy on an unverified assumption burns trust and adds a round-trip rather than removing one. Logistics questions should be pre-empted on the page where panelists land (the programme page), because the chair persona never reaches the FAQ. Visa letters and structural changes are not self-serviceable at all; the realistic win there is converting a vague multi-round thread into one complete, correctly-routed request, not deflection. The visa case is the highest-stakes: it is likely the number-one countdown email, and publishing a wrong policy answer would harm international participants two months out.
